<h2>What changed</h2><p>AWS released new minor versions for PostgreSQL 11, 12, and 13 under its Extended Support program. These versions (13.23-rds.20260819, 12.22-rds.20260820, and 11.22-rds.20260822) continue receiving critical CVE patches and bug fixes for databases that have passed their standard support end dates.</p><h2>Why this matters</h2><p>PostgreSQL major version upgrades aren't trivial—they require testing, potential application changes, and coordination across teams. Extended Support gives you a controlled runway instead of a hard cutoff. If your production workloads are still on these older versions, you can now plan upgrades on your timeline rather than AWS's, though you'll pay extra for the privilege.</p><h2>What to know</h2><p>Extended Support covers security and stability, not new features. AWS will patch critical vulnerabilities, but you're not getting performance improvements or PostgreSQL community enhancements. This is a bridge, not a destination. If you're on versions 11-13, review your upgrade path now—Extended Support buys time, but the clock is still running, and newer versions offer real improvements in performance and functionality.</p>
